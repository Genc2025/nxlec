# Q0008 clinical follow-up — 20 September 2026

## Finding and repair

The original item did not distinguish hemophilia A from hemophilia B: either factor VIII or factor IX deficiency can produce X-linked familial hemarthroses with normal PT/platelets and prolonged aPTT. This made factor IX a defensible unstated second answer even though it was not offered. The revision adds a correcting mixing study, normal factor IX activity, and normal VWF antigen/activity. The alternatives are now factor VIII, IX, XI, XIII, and VWF, each with a disease-specific rationale and source binding.

The exact 2026 USMLE outline hierarchy is recorded without NCJMM terminology. Difficulty is an author estimate (`easy`), not a psychometric result. A targeted bank search found the related reciprocal factor-IX diagnosis item Q0406; the revised Q0008 tests factor VIII after explicit IX/VWF exclusion, but exhaustive originality remains open.

## Adversarial pass

- Factor IX cannot be correct because its measured activity is normal.
- Factor XI can cause a prolonged aPTT and procedural bleeding, but is a poorer explanation for recurrent hemarthroses and the stated factor-specific exclusions.
- Factor XIII deficiency acts after fibrin formation and ordinarily does not prolong PT/aPTT.
- VWF mediates platelet adhesion and carries factor VIII, but its antigen and activity assays are normal here.
- The key does not depend only on sex, pedigree, or a screening test; the discriminating assays close the original hidden assumption.
